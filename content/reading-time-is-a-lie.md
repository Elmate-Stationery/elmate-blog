## Nobody reads at 200 words per minute the whole way through

That little "6 min read" badge at the top of every article is doing arithmetic, not measurement: word count divided by an assumed reading speed, usually somewhere between 200 and 250 words per minute. It doesn't know if paragraph four has a dense code block, whether you're skimming, or whether you got a phone call halfway through.

### So why keep it?

Because it's not actually a prediction — it's a **commitment device**. A reading-time estimate answers one question before the reader commits to anything: is this a two-minute detour or a fifteen-minute investment? That's a decision people want to make before they start scrolling, even if the number itself is soft.

### Where it actively lies

- **Code-heavy technical posts**: readers pause, copy, and test — actual time is often 2-3x the estimate
- **List-format posts**: people skim, so real time is often *less* than the estimate
- **Anything with embedded video or interactive widgets**: the estimate usually ignores them entirely

### A better framing

Instead of chasing a more "accurate" formula, treat the number as a **genre signal**, not a timer. "6 min read" tells a reader this is a focused essay, not a quick reference. "2 min read" says: this is a note, not a treatise. Set the expectation, then let the content decide the rest.
