The gap between your copy and their query

Open Search Console for any e-commerce collection page older than a year and you'll usually find the same pattern: the page ranks for terms adjacent to what it's actually optimized for, not the terms themselves.

A "Geometry Set" collection page pulling impressions for "geometry box" is not a fluke — it's a signal that the market's vocabulary and your copy's vocabulary diverged at some point, and nobody went back to reconcile them.

Read the query report like a customer, not a marketer

The fix isn't more keywords. It's fewer, better-matched ones, placed where intent actually lives:

  • H1 and title tag: match the dominant query term exactly, even if it's not your preferred internal name
  • First 100 words: use both the "official" term and the colloquial one your customers actually type
  • Bilingual markets: don't translate the term — find the term people already search in that language

Structured data closes the gap search rankings can't

Even a perfectly worded page underperforms without the schema that tells search engines what it's looking at. For product collection pages, that means:

  • Product schema for individual items where applicable
  • BreadcrumbList so the page's place in your site hierarchy is explicit
  • Accurate price range and availability where you can supply it reliably

The recurring lesson

Terminology mismatch is invisible until you go looking for it, and then it's everywhere. The fix is rarely a rewrite — it's a targeted pass through your top query report, matching what people type to what your page says, in that order.
